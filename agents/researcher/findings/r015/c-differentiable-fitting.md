## The best few, in more depth

### 1. Dress-1-to-3 (Li et al., ACM TOG / SIGGRAPH 2025)

**Work it avoids redoing.** Each optimisation iteration starts the drape from the previous iteration's drape instead of from the initial placement, and gradients come from implicit differentiation at the static state instead of finite differences. Their stated reason (verbatim): "Since the static equilibrium does not locally depend on the initial state, meaning that the Jacobian matrix of the simulated state with respect to the initial state is zero, we update the initial state of iteration n ... to the previously simulated state." They simulate "one step of 1 second to directly reach near-static equilibrium."

**Numbers, with hardware (verbatim).** "The pre-optimization steps require about 10 minutes. The garment optimization process can be finished within 2 hours on a single RTX 3090 with 24GB device memory." No iteration count is given.

**Conditions the authors state.** "To make the simulation as path-independent as possible, we avoid adding friction during the process. To prevent the bottom garments from slipping down, the boundary loop of the bottom component near the waist area is fixed" (verbatim). The pattern starts from SewFormer, and the method "cannot predict new connected pattern components if they are not included in the initial estimation" (extracted).

**Failure modes and limits they state (extracted).** Loose garments are approximated by nearby static configurations that "may not reflect the garment's true geometry"; surfaces come out over-smoothed; flipped triangles can break the next step and need a safeguard and remeshing, and naive remeshing on the body can cause penetration; optimised patterns may not follow pattern-making conventions; SewFormer gives single-layer patterns only.

**What it would take under the constraints.** The method itself cannot be run: no code is released, and the simulator is C-IPC written in Warp and timed on an RTX 3090. Two of its conditions can be rebuilt inside Blender without any of its code. The first is friction off during fitting drapes (cloth-body and self-collision friction to zero) with the waistband pinned to stop slip, then one final drape with friction back on. The second is the warm start, which the repository already holds as `entries/warm-start-repeated-drapes.md`; Dress-1-to-3 is a published instance of that entry and states the same validity condition the entry marks as untested (the equilibrium must not depend on where the drape starts). The equilibrium-adjoint gradient needs a solver whose stiffness matrix is exposed, which Blender's is not.

**Test-bed problem it addresses.** The sign-unstable width sensitivities (friction off is the authors' own remedy for path dependence) and the per-step drape cost (warm start). Their target is a set of generated multi-view images, not one reference photo's landmarks and silhouette widths.

### 2. Wang, Rule-Free Sewing Pattern Adjustment (ACM TOG 2018) — background

**Work it avoids redoing.** The inner quasistatic simulation is not run to convergence at every outer step. Verbatim: "we propose to approximate the quasistatic state by using a smaller S, the number of simulation iterations per outer iteration ... after M inexact outer iterations, e.g., M = 32, we run one exact outer iteration with sufficient simulation accuracy. If the total cost fails to drop since the last exact iteration, we increase the accuracy of quasistatic simulation, decrease the step length, and relaunch the optimization from the last exact outer iteration. In the worst case, our inexact two-step approach is reduced to the exact approach." The outer step is preconditioned and compared against plain gradient descent and Gauss–Newton in the paper's figures.

**Numbers, with hardware (verbatim).** Experiments ran on "an Intel Core i7-5820K 3.3GHz processor and an NVIDIA GeForce GTX TITAN X Graphics Card", using Intel MKL PARDISO on CPU and CUDA on GPU. "The system is able to sufficiently reduce the objective within 256 to 512 iterations." A teaser caption: "the whole optimization process lasts less than two minutes for a mesh with 66K elements."

**Conditions the authors state (verbatim).** "We propose to ignore cloth-body friction and self collision of cloth. We believe it is a reasonable assumption for sewing pattern adjustment, since we prefer garments to drape naturally and relaxedly into a unique configuration." And: "ignoring self collision does not prevent the system from handling multi-layered garments, such as the pants example in Fig. 14, as long as self collision does not greatly affect the draped garment shape." The tangent stiffness matrix "must be evaluable and invertible."

**Failure modes and limits they state (verbatim).** "While we prefer the quasistatic state to be unique, it is often not, due to the local minima. We typically observe this issue as dents being stuck during the parameter optimization process ... This issue can be resolved by re-initializing quasistatic simulation, or using larger bending forces." The paper's main stated limitation is dependence on the accuracy of the body model. The overall reduction of the objective "depends on the quality of the initialization."

**What it would take under the constraints.** No code, and the speed comes from its own GPU quasistatic solver. The inexact strategy itself needs no gradients from the solver and fits the test bed's Gauss–Newton/Levenberg–Marquardt/Broyden loop: shorter warm-started Blender drapes for most steps, a full fresh drape every few steps as the check, and backtracking from the last full drape when the cost has not dropped. The friction-and-self-collision-off condition is the same one Dress-1-to-3 arrived at seven years later. For a tunic worn over trousers with a scarf on top, self-collision does affect shape, so turning it off is outside Wang's own stated condition and would need the full arm as a check.

**Test-bed problem it addresses.** Every step being a real drape (inexact inner solve with a safeguard), and non-unique drapes (named directly, with two remedies).

### 3. Sensitive Couture (Umetani, Kaufman, Igarashi, Grinspun, ACM TOG 2011) — background

**Work it avoids redoing.** It predicts the new drape from drapes already computed. Verbatim: "SC can draw upon two kinds of readily reusable data: the draped configuration x0 ... (i.e., zeroth-order data) at the previous pointer position d0 ..., and the sensitivities s1 = ∂x/∂d1, s2 = ∂x/∂d2 of the draped configurations xm ... (i.e., first-order data) at the cached previous configurations dm (m = 1, ..., M)." These are blended with generalised moving least squares, and "the current sensitivity configuration warm starts the next time-integration cycle." Sensitivities are exact, from the stiffness matrix at the static state (the 3n × 2n "design sensitivity matrix").

**Numbers, with hardware (verbatim, Table 1).** "All timings were measured on an Intel Core 2 Duo 2.66 GHz laptop, 4Gb memory." For five sessions of about 1,543–1,682 coarse nodes and 5,032–10,168 fine nodes: sensitivity computation averaged 123–233 ms and GMLS interpolation averaged 0.36–0.40 ms.

**Conditions the authors state (verbatim).** "Buckling, wrinkling, and static friction all make for a nonlinear relation between the 2D design and the 3D draped form. While linear sensitivity is a good first step, we found that for the larger edits typical at the start of the design process, or for radical redesigns, the local model produced by sensitivity analysis ... does not remain valid for the full extent of the editing operation." Hence the cache of several linearisations rather than one.

**Failure modes and limits they state (verbatim).** "For a nonlinear cloth statics problem, there exist configurations for which the stiffness matrix is close to singular", for example thin cloth that cannot hold itself up; their remedy is to "recompute S with bending stiffness corresponding to h = 1/100", a stiffer bending used only for the sensitivity, "and does not affect the bending stiffness used in determining the ultimate static drape." Editing more than two degrees of freedom from one drag is left as future work.

**What it would take under the constraints.** Blender does not expose the stiffness matrix, so the exact sensitivities are out of reach; the finite-difference sensitivities the test bed already measures stand in for them. The rest is a few hundred lines of NumPy on CPU: store each earlier fit's (cut, landmark values, measured Jacobian), and predict the response at a new cut by moving least squares over both the values and the slopes, weighted by distance in control space. This is a more careful version of the pooled model that already cut drapes by about 44% for lengths. The paper's own condition explains where it should fail: where buckling or static friction is in play, which is where the width sensitivities flipped sign. The stiffer-bending-for-sensitivity-only trick suggests a cheap experiment for widths: measure width sensitivities on drapes with raised bending stiffness and check whether their sign agreement across runs rises.

**Test-bed problem it addresses.** Reuse of earlier fits (a pooled sensitivity prior, across runs), and a stated reason why width sensitivities would not pool.

### 4. DiffCloth (Li, Du, Wu, Xu, Matusik, ACM TOG 2022) — background

**Work it avoids redoing.** Gradients replace sampled search in cloth inverse problems; the part kept here is its study of when gradients stop being useful.

**Numbers and findings (verbatim).** On a cloth falling onto a sphere at four offsets: "as establishing and breaking contact becomes more frequent, the local landscape tends to be bumpier." The ranking of damage to gradients: "the branches in the contact conditions only introduce non-smoothness and maintain continuity; contact surface discretization creates discontinuities due to the jumps of normals across adjacent triangles, but we still expect continuity almost everywhere; adding or deleting nodes in the contact set creates frequent and the most severe discontinuities due to the introduction or removal of impulsive forces." On a one-parameter friction identification: "the landscape of the loss function is bumpy due to the large variation in the collision set as a function of the frictional coefficient ... evolutionary strategies can achieve a lower final loss because the samples can freely explore the full range of frictional contact, while L-BFGS-B can get trapped in a local optimum." Hardware for its timings was not located in the text read.

**Conditions the authors state.** Gradients pay off most as the number of decision variables grows; with few parameters and a bumpy landscape, sampling can win (the friction example above). A constant contact set "does not cause too much trouble for gradient computation regardless of its size" (verbatim).

**Failure modes and limits.** The contact-set discontinuity is identified, not solved; the paper points to differentiable rendering for possible future fixes.

**What it would take under the constraints.** MIT-licensed C++ with Python bindings; its README lists Mac OS 12 as tested, and Apple Silicon is not mentioned, so a build on the test bed's machine is plausible but unverified. It is not a garment-on-body fitting tool. The usable part needs no code: a flared panel's width changes which nodes touch the body and the other cloth, exactly the contact-set changes DiffCloth ranks worst, so a finite difference across such a change measures a jump, not a slope. With only a few width controls, DiffCloth's own result favours sampling over gradient steps for those controls (a short bracketing search, or finite differences averaged over several seeds and step sizes), while lengths keep the Gauss–Newton/Broyden treatment.

**Test-bed problem it addresses.** The sign-unstable width sensitivities: a measured mechanism (contact-set changes) and a measured remedy class (sampling for low-dimensional bumpy problems).

### 5. PhysAvatar (ECCV 2024) and MPMAvatar (NeurIPS 2025), together

**Work it avoids redoing.** Nothing; both fall back on finite differences around a non-differentiable simulator, as the test bed does. They are here because they show what the field does when it has no usable gradient.

**Numbers, with hardware (extracted).** PhysAvatar: forward differences on 3 scalars (density, membrane and bending stiffness) with step sizes 5, 0.05 and 0.05; Adam for 100 iterations; δt = 0.04 over 24-frame clips; the Codim-IPC garment simulation "runs around 0.05−1 fps on the CPU". By its Algorithm 1 that is about four rollouts per iteration, which the paper does not state outright. MPMAvatar: finite differences "following PhysAvatar" on 3 parameters with perturbations 0.05, 5 and 0.005; Adam for 200 iterations; about 1.1 s per simulated frame on "a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090". Neither reports total fitting time.

**Conditions the authors state.** PhysAvatar chose forward differences because the problem "involves only three scalar parameters" and C-IPC "lacks an implementation of accurate analytical gradients." MPMAvatar, Appendix D (extracted as quoted segments): the scheme "scales linearly with the number of parameters", is practical because "per-garment material parameters suffice due to limited intra-garment heterogeneity", and "incorporating differentiable simulators ... may improve scalability."

**Failure modes and limits.** Neither discusses noise, smoothing, or sign stability of the finite-difference estimates. PhysAvatar needs dense multi-view capture and manual segmentation; its garment is a scanned mesh, not a sewing pattern.

**What it would take under the constraints.** Codim-IPC is Apache-2.0 C++ and runs on CPU, slowly; PhysAvatar's own code was promised, not found. In practice nothing here needs running: it confirms that finite differences over a handful of parameters, with the solver treated as a black box, is accepted practice in 2024–2025 garment work, and that no one in this line has reported how stable those estimates are.

**Test-bed problem it addresses.** The finite-difference Jacobian itself, as a baseline; it offers no answer to the width instability.

### 6. DiffAvatar (CVPR 2024), with DiffXPBD (PACMCGIT 2023) as its engine

**Work it avoids redoing.** The extra drapes per control: one adjoint (backward) pass gives the gradient with respect to every pattern control at once. Verbatim: "In each iteration, we run a dynamic differentiable simulation until the garments reach quasi-equilibrium state with the current set of parameters θ to obtain a draped garment ... the gradient information dϕ/dθ is obtained through back-propagation using the differentiable simulator." The pattern is moved through a 2D control cage with mean value coordinates.

**Numbers, with hardware (verbatim).** "Implemented in C++ using open-source libraries such as Eigen and libigl. All experiments were conducted on a machine with 14-core i7 CPU with 32GB RAM ... Optimization takes about one minute per iteration and total times vary between 20 to 200 minutes depending on the garment and iteration count ... Our method runs on CPU, whereas the baselines are run on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4080 GPU." The optimiser is plain gradient descent. DiffXPBD (extracted, Table 1, AMD Threadripper PRO 3975WX, 32 cores): for a 14,639-vertex T-shirt, about 0.079 s forward and 0.496 s differentiable forward per step; memory grows linearly with simulation length because every step's state is stored.

**Conditions the authors state (extracted).** Relies on near-quasi-equilibrium states, so garments with strong friction or dynamic effects are hard; starts from a template per garment category and does not handle discrete changes in panel count or topology. Ablation: without the control cage the optimiser produces inverted triangles that make the simulator fail; without seam-length regularisation seams mismatch and fabric gathers.

**Failure modes and limits.** Multi-layer clothing under occlusion is called fundamentally difficult. DiffXPBD inherits XPBD's slow convergence for very stiff constraints and checks its derivatives only by matrix symmetry and row sums, with no gradient-error measurement (extracted).

**What it would take under the constraints.** The paper shows a differentiable garment fit at a usable speed on a CPU, which removes the CUDA objection in principle. But no code is released for either paper, and rebuilding DiffXPBD-style gradients means writing an XPBD garment solver outside Blender (for example in Warp's macOS CPU path or in plain C++), whose drape would no longer be the drape the test bed measures. The control cage carries over without a differentiable solver: driving the pattern's widths and lengths through a few cage handles is a smaller, better-conditioned control space for any finite-difference or sampling scheme.

**Test-bed problem it addresses.** The cost of the finite-difference Jacobian (in principle, through a different solver), and conditioning of the pattern controls.

## What the area doesn't offer

No paper in this area measures what the test bed measured: whether a sensitivity of a draped garment keeps its sign across runs or across designs. Wang (2018) says plainly that quasistatic drapes are "often not" unique, Sensitive Couture says buckling, wrinkling and static friction break linear prediction, and DiffCloth shows contact-set changes make cloth losses bumpy, but none quantifies seed-to-seed spread of a fitted garment or treats fold bifurcations as something to model; the textile drape-test literature reports that one fabric gives different fold counts on repeated tests (search snippets only, not read), which is the physical version of the same thing. The remedies offered are all side-steps: drop friction and self-collision so the drape becomes a function of the cut (Wang 2018; Dress-1-to-3 2025), stiffen bending only for the sensitivity computation (Sensitive Couture), or sample instead of differentiate when parameters are few (DiffCloth). Randomised smoothing and local bundles of perturbed rollouts are developed only for rigid-body robotics (Suh 2022, Le Lidec 2022, Scheffler 2026, Aditya 2026), not for cloth on a body. Reuse of sensitivities across separate fits, which the test bed already does, appears only as Sensitive Couture's within-session cache from 2011; no 2023–2026 paper learns a prior on sensitivities from earlier fits or warm-starts an optimiser from earlier garments, apart from starting the pattern at a learned estimate. Differentiable garment simulators with body contact and self-collision are either unreleased (DiffAvatar, DiffXPBD, Dress-1-to-3, Yu et al.) or CUDA-bound, and the open engines that run on a Mac CPU do not differentiate full cloth (Newton marks VBD and Style3D cloth "❌" for differentiability; Genesis does not document it). The image-to-pattern models (ChatGarment, AIpparel, Design2GarmentCode, SewingLDM, GarmentDiffusion, DressCode, NGL-Prompter, DressWild) stop at the estimate with no refinement against the photo, and NGL-Prompter reports that VLMs "struggle to reason about numeric garment parameters" and that "GarmentCode has a large number of parameters and only some of them are used to generate the final pattern", so the step the test bed spends its drapes on is not covered by that line; the only single-image method with a simulation refinement is Dress-1-to-3, whose targets are generated multi-view images rather than one photo's landmarks. None of the papers read evaluates very wide flared trousers or a long free-hanging scarf, the garments where folds and contact changes would be expected to matter most.
